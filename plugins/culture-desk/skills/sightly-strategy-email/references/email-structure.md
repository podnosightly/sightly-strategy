# Strategy email: answer-the-brief checklist, section template, worked skeleton

## Two modes

- **Internal handoff** (common): to a seller or AE before a client meeting. Carries the "honest read"
  and "what I need / who to loop in" sections, can name teammates and budget mechanics, and is candid
  about what's sourced and what's directional.
- **Client-facing note:** drops the internal sections, holds tighter to the hard-rules terminology,
  and never presents something directional as if it were measured.

## The voice benchmark

Match the strategist's own recent emails of the same kind for structure, length and density; don't
lift sentences. With the Gmail connector, read two or three of their recent sent handoffs in full.
Without it, ask for one, or follow `02_Voice_and_Terminology.md` › How Sightly writes a
recommendation. The client-email exemplars in `sightly-brand-voice` are secondary.

## Answer-the-brief checklist (run against the original request)

Go through the requester's actual ask line by line and mark each ANSWERED, PARTIAL or MISSING before
finalizing. The email exists to answer these, not to recite Sightly's story.

- [ ] Reach to the client's stated audience, in their words, across the named walled gardens.
- [ ] Every named segment covered, including the awkward one (an audio or podcast audience reached
      through contextual video, a demo we can only speak to directionally). Name how, or flag why
      not.
- [ ] "Put it into action": the activation story, not just the insight.
- [ ] Grow the audience: answered, framed as the plan's move if the lift is unproven.
- [ ] Keep it fresh for existing fans: answered (usually the retention persona).
- [ ] Show up in unique ways: answered with 2–3 concrete plays, not a restatement of the method.
- [ ] Any look-back requested (what popped last season or period): delivered, mapped to this year.
- [ ] The seller's meta-asks (what's missing, who to loop in): answered.

Anything MISSING or PARTIAL either gets built or gets an honest one-line flag with the fix.

## Section template

A short header (a word or a short phrase), short plain paragraphs under it. Adapt to the brief; drop
sections it doesn't call for.

1. **Opening.** Greeting. Deck or plan links up top when they exist. One line on what this is, and
   one line orienting the reader ("below is the argument, your questions answered, and what I
   need").
2. **Strategy.** The tension in two or three dense sentences, then what sets the plan apart, led by
   what Sightly measured from its own data. One business-as-usual comparison stated as fact.
3. **The look-back.** If the client asked what worked last season or period: the beats that moved
   the conversation, each with a real number and its source, each mapped to what we surround now.
4. **Who we reach, in the client's terms.** Map our targeting to their stated audience segment by
   segment. Where content-level reach is the proof (video-level suitability, the filtered universe),
   say so and bridge it back to their language. Flag directional segments.
5. **Personas.** Each: name, tension or profile, where and how reached. The retention persona
   answers "keep it fresh"; the growth persona answers "grow the audience."
6. **Moments and Brand Suitability.** The moments we surround (real, named) and what Brand
   Suitability blocks. Explain the boards and SmartLists mechanism once.
7. **Unique ways to show up.** Two or three concrete plays the client will see in market.
8. **The honest read (internal).** Sourced (sell it) and directional (the plan's move). The one or
   two flags for the room. Internal candor, not client copy.
9. **What I need / who to loop in (internal).** From the client: missing inputs (dates, budget,
   KPI, managed or self-service, whether a channel is in scope). On our side: what the next step
   needs. Timeline.
10. **Close.** End on the specific piece worth pressure-testing when there is one; otherwise "Happy
    to walk through any of it." Sign off as the strategist.

## Dedup

One point, one stat, one place. The same video-level stat under both "who we reach" and a trailing
"targeting" section, or the same moment named twice, is the classic duplication in a merged draft.
Sweep before the voice pass.

## Then: dedup, voice, claims

The skeleton is content and structure. Dedup, then run the voice check and the claim check in the
skill's Step 4 before it ships, and re-check after any edit made during that pass.
