# Sightly Strategy Email

Turn a brief or a worked strategy into a finished email. Two modes: an internal handoff to a seller
or AE walking into a client meeting (the common case), or a client-facing note. Long-form prose, not
slide copy.

The failure this prevents: an email that tells Sightly's story more completely than it answers the
questions the requester actually asked. Answer their questions first, in their terms.

Read `references/email-structure.md` before writing. It carries the answer-the-brief checklist, the
section template and a worked skeleton. If the Culture Desk knowledge base is present, read
`02_Voice_and_Terminology.md` › How Sightly writes a recommendation and `13_When_Asked_to_Build.md` ›
Client emails too. They govern where this skill and the knowledge base differ.

---

## Step 1: gather before writing

- **The substance.** The strategy, brief or data pull this email is about. If a strategy has
  already been worked in the thread, use it. Do not invent numbers, rates or proof points. Leave
  anything unknown (budget, flight dates) as an open ask, never a guess.
- **The exact ask, verbatim.** Get the original request and list every question in it. Each
  question maps to a section below. This is the most-skipped step, and skipping it is what produces
  a Sightly-story email that never answers the client.
- **The voice benchmark.** The strategist's own recent emails of the same kind, to match structure,
  length and density, not to lift sentences. If the Gmail connector is available, read two or three
  of the strategist's recent sent handoffs in full. If not, ask the strategist for one, or follow the
  patterns in `02` alone. The client-email exemplars in `sightly-brand-voice` are secondary.
- **Mode.** Internal handoff or client-facing. Internal carries the "what I need / who to loop in"
  close and can name teammates and budget mechanics. Client-facing drops those and holds tighter to
  the hard-rules terminology below.

## Step 2: answer the brief, in the client's terms

Run the answer-the-brief checklist in the reference against the original ask. Mark every explicit
question ANSWERED, PARTIAL or MISSING before finalizing, phrased in the client's own words. The
recurring ones on a franchise or reach pitch:

- **Reach to their stated audience** across the named walled gardens, and "put it into action": the
  activation, not just the insight. Cover every named segment, including the awkward one (a
  podcast-listener audience answered through contextual video; a demo you can only speak to
  directionally). Name how, or flag why not.
- **The franchise asks, each answered separately:** grow the audience, keep it fresh for existing
  fans, show up in unique ways. Make "unique ways" concrete with two or three plays the client will
  see.
- **Any look-back requested** (what popped last season or period), each moment mapped to what we
  surround this time.
- **The seller's meta-asks:** what's missing, who to loop in.

Anything MISSING or PARTIAL gets built, or gets an honest one-line flag with the fix. A flagged gap
beats a confident dodge.

## Step 3: structure

Greeting, deck or plan links at the very top if they exist, then short section headers with short
plain paragraphs. Use the sections the brief calls for and drop the rest; the full template and a
worked skeleton are in the reference. Match the length of the strategist's own comparable email:
answer what was asked, and keep the supporting analysis in the deck or the backing document unless
the recipient asked for it.

- **The opportunity.** What the brand wants and why now.
- **Strategy.** The tension, the data with exact numbers and their consequence, the mechanism, and
  one business-as-usual comparison stated as fact (`02` › How Sightly writes a recommendation). Lead
  with what Sightly measured from its own data.
- **The look-back,** if asked for. Dated beats, exact figures, each mapped to what we surround now.
- **Audience and personas.** The funded audience from Brand Mentality, proposal-stage until a real
  Persona Builder run. One sentence per persona: what it is and where it's reached.
- **How it runs.** Activation in plain terms: channels, Brand Suitability, SmartLists, Anticipation
  Boards, the Brand Profile.
- **One thing to flag,** placed next to the point it qualifies: one focused caveat with detail.
- **The honest read** (internal only). What's sourced from our data (sell it) and what's directional
  (frame it as the plan's move, not a finding).
- **What I need** (internal handoff only): what you'd need to cost it (budget, flight window, KPI,
  blockers), how to sharpen the ask to the client, and who to loop in. Don't invent teammate names
  or owners; leave a placeholder or ask.
- **Close.** End on the specific piece worth pressure-testing when there is one; otherwise a plain
  "Happy to walk through any of it."

Map every question from Step 1 to a section, and answer the named audience segments in the client's
own words, not only as Sightly's method.

## Step 4: dedup, then voice and claims, then prove it

- **Dedup.** One point, one stat, one place. A merged or edited draft is where duplication creeps
  in: the same stat under two sections, the same moment named twice. Sweep for it first.
- **Voice.** Run `sightly-brand-voice`'s Gate 1 self-audit on the draft (banned patterns, the
  negation-pivot family, AI tells, the copy rules in `02`). If the strategist has a personal voice
  skill installed, run it too. Where the session can spawn a fresh agent, give it the draft and the
  benchmark email and ask it to quote every line that reads performed or AI-written; a cold read
  catches what the writer's own pass defends.
- **Claims.** Run `sightly-claim-integrity` on any interpretive claim before it goes to a client.
- **Re-check after every edit,** including your own last-minute rewrite. New prose written after the
  last check is where tells and errors re-enter.
- **Verify before delivery:** every fact traces to the strategy or brief, every question in the ask
  is answered, the terminology below holds, sample-bound numbers carry their denominator ("in our
  tracked sample"), and the email is no longer than it needs to be.

## Step 5: deliver

Hand over the finished email plus a short coverage note: which brief questions are answered, which
are flagged directional, and the "what I need" list. Offer to put it in a Gmail draft on the original
thread, and never send it without being asked. If it runs long, offer a leaner cut.

## Sightly terminology and compliance (always on)

The source of truth is the live `01_Hard_Rules.md` and `02_Voice_and_Terminology.md` in the Culture
Desk project. Where this section and those files differ, follow those files.

Marks (Rule 24): SmartLists and Anticipation Software are always plain, with no trademark or
registered mark. The boards are "Anticipation Boards." Brand Mentality is plain in every email; it
takes the ® only once, at its first mention in a proposal. "Brand Suitability" is the default term,
and "brand safety" is fine when the client or RFP uses it (Rule 22). Use "Social Intelligence" in
anything external; "social listening" is prohibited there (Rule 23). For YouTube, say "Google Premier
Partner" with "Non-Public API Access." Never use "YouTube Measurement Program" or "YTMP" in any
material (Rule 21). For TikTok, say "badged TikTok Marketing Partner, Media Buying Technology
category" (Rule 25).

Never name Sightly's own competitors client-facing ("other offerings in the market"). Name the
advertiser's category competitors wherever the work calls for it (Rule 9). IAS and DoubleVerify can
be named as third-party verification integrations that Sightly works alongside. Never describe them
as competitors. Never name white-label partners (Rule 2). IRIS.TV and Spectrum Reach are the only
nameable CTV partners, always under the Sightly / Brand Mentality Premium CTV umbrella (Rule 3). Never mention GARM.

No invented proof points, rates or benchmarks; check every stat against the project's do-not-use
register and the do-not-use list in `08_Proof_Points_and_Battle_Cards.md`, and keep any hedge the
source applied. Match each claim to the real platform mechanism (for example, a programmatic housing
buy is governed by Google's HEC restricted-targeting policy on DV360, not Meta's "Special Ad
Category").

## What this skill does not do

- It does not write slide copy or build the deck.
- It does not send anything. It drafts, and offers a Gmail draft on the thread.
- It does not invent numbers, owners or teammate names.
