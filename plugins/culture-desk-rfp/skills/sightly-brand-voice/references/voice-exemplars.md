# Voice exemplars — Dan Podnos

Real emails Dan sent to sellers. These are the ground truth for his voice in long-form prose.
Match the rhythm and the structure; do not lift the sentences.

**Scope.** These apply to emails, internal notes and written strategy summaries. They do **not**
apply to slide copy. They are long-form emails, and their register produces sprawl inside a slide’s
word budget. Slide copy follows the deck-builder skills for structure, `sightly-visual-brand` for
design tokens, and the copy rules in SKILL.md for the sentences.

## What to extract

**Named openers followed immediately by a working sentence.** "The opportunity." "The Sightly
Solution." "Why Option 3 leads." "Two personas, built from Brand Mentality." Never a bare label
standing alone as the whole beat.

**Long sentences that carry causation.** "Montblanc’s August date is the smart move, with less
competitive auction, lower CPMs, and Explorer Infinite gets room to breathe as the discovery
fragrance of summer before Q4 noise kicks in." The length exists because the clauses are doing work.

**Explicit comparison against business as usual.** "A standard fragrance YouTube buy puts Men 18-45
on a flat CPM and calls it a day." "Scott’s BAU plan is driving ~20.5M imps and we’re not
positioning this as more volume." He names what the alternative does badly, in plain terms.

**"One thing to flag"** is his construction for raising a caveat.

**A recommendation with its reason, in its own beat.** "Option 1 is the cleanest client-facing
answer: one Sightly product, one CPM, reach-led delivery."

**Numbers with units and consequence attached.** "£105,888 @ £8.50 CPM → ~12,457,412 impressions."
"13 weeks from first search to booking." Never a bare percentage.

**Closes that name the thing worth pressure-testing.** "Happy to jump on a quick call and pressure
test the Anticipation Board examples as that’s the piece that wins this."

**Deck and plan links at the very top**, as a short bulleted pair, before any prose.

## What he never does

- Bare label fragments as section headers. "The opportunity." always has a sentence after it.
- Comma-stacked fragment cadence ("One file, two plans, built to split").
- Aphorisms or slogans ("we’re not selling reach, we’re selling compression").
- Invented shorthand presented as established terminology.
- Status-field phrasing ("Numbers are directional until we hear budget").

## Exemplar 1 — Cartier, to Liv

Hey Liv,

Strategy deck + Media Plan attached.

Keeping this tight based on what Scott asked for.

Core strategy (what we give them vs BAU YouTube):
Scott’s BAU plan is driving ~20.5M imps and we’re not positioning this as "more volume." We’re
positioning Sightly as the managed, in-flight intelligence layer: persona-led targeting, contextual
SmartList curation, and real-time moments monitoring/rotation, so every impression is higher quality
and culturally timed.

Our story is: BAU buys scale. A Sightly-managed campaign buys smarter luxury reach, real-time
cultural relevance, and in-flight optimisation into the moments these HNW audiences are already
leaning into.

Anticipation Boards (the "lean-in" moments that win this)
Met Gala is the obvious example, but the value lies in the quieter, high-affinity luxury moments
that are meaningful to this audience and actionable mid-flight.

Option 1 is the cleanest client-facing answer: one Sightly product, one CPM, reach-led delivery,
while still giving us a controlled pocket to optimise toward view-through where the fables are
performing.

If helpful, happy to jump on a quick call and pressure test the Anticipation Board examples as
that’s the piece that wins this.

Thanks,
Dan

## Exemplar 2 — Montblanc, to Liv (the fullest example of his structure)

Hey Liv,

Deck and plan linked.
- Montblanc Strategy Deck
- Montblanc Media Plan

The opportunity. Montblanc’s August date is the smart move, with less competitive auction, lower
CPMs, and Explorer Infinite gets room to breathe as the discovery fragrance of summer before Q4
noise kicks in. Explorer Extreme already primes the franchise on Father’s Day. This August launch
builds on that. And the brief flags a Christmas campaign coming, where Option 3 is designed to feed
directly into that brief with 20 weeks of audience data and SmartList intelligence already built.

One thing to flag: the brief rightly called out that consumers will be travelling in August. Our
approach is to follow them where they live digitally, with Custom Intent on fragrance research and
discovery behaviours, and explicitly exclude travel retail, duty-free, and airport content at the
video level. Online purchase intent only.

The Sightly Solution. A standard fragrance YouTube buy puts Men 18-45 on a flat CPM and calls it a
day. This Brand Mentality plan separates two jobs because a man discovering a new cologne and a
woman researching Christmas gifts are not in the same mindset and shouldn’t be bought the same way.

CPM builds the audience. CPV qualifies the audience. Brand Mentality decides where Explorer Infinite
shows up and rotates SmartLists every time a competitor launches or a gifting moment spikes.

Why Option 3 leads. It’s the choice that lets Montblanc own the Christmas gifting window rather than
just turn up for it. The Christmas brief coming next is the natural third act, and Option 3 creates
the SmartList intelligence and audience data that makes that brief significantly cheaper to
activate.

SmartLists are the differentiator. Real-time, contextually curated YouTube inventory lists built per
persona, rotated in-flight as moments spike. Exclusions across every line: duty-free, travel retail,
budget dupes, counterfeit fragrance content, "smells like" dupe creators. Protected at the video
level, not category-level blocking that strips reach in the lifestyle and luxury environments
Montblanc actually needs to be in.

Measurement. Brand Lift Study from Week 2 (Options 2 + 3), Ad Recall + Consideration, split by
persona. Weekly: reach, frequency, CPM/CPV by line, Board fires, SmartList refresh count, view rate
by asset. Budget shifts in-flight to whichever persona-and-moment combinations outperform. Allocation
isn’t locked in advance, the data does it.

Happy to walk through before it goes out.

Thanks,
Dan

## Exemplar 3 — Tesco, to Giles

Hi Giles - hope you are well.

I wanted to pass along the rough outline of the deck, which you can find here, where I’ve integrated
our sentiment analysis via infographic where possible. Many resulting slides are duplicative and
will be removed.

I’ll wait for your feedback before sending a final email detailing the strategy, but here is an
overview:

Objective
Proactively mitigate negative sentiment around "Profiteering" and "Farming" by injecting Tesco’s
positive, transparent messaging directly into conversations shaping public perception, aiming to
shift Brand Consideration and Favourability.

The Approach: 30/70 Direct vs Adjacent Targeting
Using Sightly’s Anticipation Boards, we will track the real-time velocity of the "Farming" and
"Profiteering" narratives across Reddit, TikTok, YouTube and news to power a two-tier targeting
strategy.

30% Direct (Tesco-Specific Negativity). We actively target users engaging with content that
explicitly criticises Tesco. Scale here will fluctuate with daily viral spikes, but this tier lets us
confront the most vocal critics head-on with proof-based messaging.

70% Adjacent (Macro Themes). We target the broader, always-on conversations around the Cost of
Living Crisis, UK Supermarket Profits, and Support UK Farmers, where Tesco may not always be named,
but where perceptions of all "big supermarkets" are being formed. This ensures we reach a much
larger audience whose views are being passively shaped by these macro narratives, without being
constrained by Tesco-specific scale.

Looking forward to hearing your thoughts.

Best regards,
Dan
