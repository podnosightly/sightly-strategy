# Sightly Brand Voice, Tone, and Writing Standards

*Single source of truth for voice, tone, terminology, banned patterns, messaging pillars, and proof points.*

*Consolidated from: Matt's Master Playbook Part 2, Dan's Brand Voice Guidelines, Dan's Copy Guide, Dan's Strategy Copy Guide.*

## Tone

Professional, confident, sharp, strategic, clear, grounded in execution, persuasive but not exaggerated, polished but not bloated. The tone should feel like an experienced media strategist speaking to clients, internal leadership, or cross-functional partners. Sightly should sound consultative, forward-thinking, data-informed, and contextually intelligent. Never like a generic ad tech platform, a vague AI company, or a basic managed service partner.

## Voice Characteristics

**Confident and evidence-grounded.** Claims are almost always backed by a proof point. The voice does not speculate; it cites.

**Platform-savvy but not jargony.** The goal is accessible precision ("smarter, faster, safer media decisions") over dense ad-tech shorthand.

**Cultural immediacy as a core tone marker.** "Speed of culture" is a recurring brand phrase and de facto tagline territory. Variants: "move at the speed of culture," "react at the speed of culture," "operate at the speed of culture."

**Consultative, not transactional.** Managed service is framed as "white glove" and "extension of your team." The relationship is strategic partnership, not vendor.

**Proprietary language as voice differentiators.** Brand Mentality®, Anticipation Software®, and SmartLists™ serve as brand voice anchors that signal patented differentiation.

## Copy Rules (enforced on all output)

1. The first sentence of every paragraph carries the idea. Do not warm up, restate the brief, or signal what you are about to say. Lead with the point, not the setup.
2. One job per sentence. If a sentence contains two ideas joined by a dash or "but," split it or cut the weaker half.
3. Specific beats generic. "13 weeks from first search to booking" is useful. "A long consideration journey" is not. Prefer named behaviors, actual numbers, real platforms.
4. Plain punctuation. Use commas and periods. Colons and semicolons are fine when they carry meaning, but do not use them for rhythm.
5. No AI cadence tells. No "X. Y. Z." triplet headlines. No "Not just X, but Y." No "It's not X, it's Y." No "That's why / that's how" pivots. No fragment stacks used as a beat.
6. Bullets are sentences. Bold a real data point or named behavior inside the sentence, not a label at the front. If the bold is removable without changing the meaning, it is a label; rewrite.
7. Soft word budget for deck slides: strategy and persona slides run 80-130 words. Proof, cover, and close slides can run shorter. If a slide breaks 140, cut. If it runs under 40, it is too thin for the argument it is trying to make.
8. Setup then payoff per slide. Each slide opens with one sentence that frames the idea, then pays it off with evidence. Never drop into a bullet list cold.
9. Three line wraps maximum per paragraph.
10. No orphan words (single word alone on a line). Fix the break or rewrite the line.

## Banned Patterns and Language

**Never use:**
- Em dashes used for rhythm or drama (use commas, colons, semicolons, or clean sentence breaks)
- Hyphens as sentence breaks
- The phrase "trade areas"
- Agency buzzwords: "best-in-class," "cutting-edge," "innovative solutions," "disruptive," "holistic ecosystem," "360-degree approach," "leverage synergies"
- Generic cliches: "move the needle," "at the end of the day"
- Hollow intensifiers: "crucial," "vital," "pivotal," "game-changing," "transformative"
- Triplet patterns ("X. Y. Z." headlines, "X, Y, Z" parallel phrases)
- "It's not X, it's Y" constructions
- "Not just X but Y" constructions
- "That's why / that's how" pivots
- Throat-clearing openers ("In today's landscape," "It's no secret," "When it comes to")
- Bold-label bullets ("Reach: We reach people.")
- Over-explaining obvious points when concise language will do
- Robotic or overly templated phrasing
- Empty storytelling not connected to the recommendation
- Strategy language that sounds inflated, generic, or unsupported
- Writing that sounds like it came from a junior strategist trying too hard
- "We believe" or "we feel" (use "here is what this does and why")

**Lean into:**
- Specific recommendations
- Clear rationale
- Strategic framing tied to execution
- Language that reflects media fluency
- Thoughtful positioning of tradeoffs
- Tight client-facing prose and crisp internal communication
- Confident recommendation language
- Writing that sounds informed, commercially aware, and credible

## Preferred Phrasing Library

Use these constructions when they fit naturally. Do not overuse.

- "Our recommendation is designed to..."
- "This approach allows us to..."
- "Built to align with the client's stated priorities around..."
- "Gives us a strong way to..."
- "Keeps the plan focused, efficient, and scalable..."
- "Balances reach with relevance..."
- "Helps align messaging with..."
- "Supports more efficient delivery against..."
- "Grounded in the audience behaviors and content environments most relevant to..."
- "Creates a clearer role for each platform within the broader strategy..."

## Terminology — Approved and Prohibited

### Approved / Preferred
- "Brand Suitability" (use instead of "brand safety" in all external materials)
- "Social Intelligence" (use instead of "social listening" in all external materials)
- "Brand Mentality®" (always with registered mark, treat as proper noun)
- "Anticipation Software®" (same treatment)
- "SmartLists™" (with trademark symbol)
- "Speed of Culture" (recurring brand phrase, de facto tagline territory)
- "Cultural intelligence" / "contextual intelligence" (acceptable umbrella terms)
- "Brand Risk" or "Risk Avoidance" (approved alternatives to "brand safety")
- "Over 150K news sources" (how to describe the news data partner)
- YouTube: "badged YouTube Measurement Program (YTMP) partner" (must include "badged" and "YTMP")
- TikTok: "badged TikTok Marketing Partner, Media Buying Technology category"
- IRIS.TV: always lead with Sightly; IRIS.TV is "the data partner" not the product name

### Prohibited
- "Brand Safety" (prohibited in all external-facing materials)
- "Social Listening" (prohibited due to data partnership sensitivity)
- Competitor names in client-facing collateral (use "Sightly's offering vs. other offerings in the market")
- Infegy (social data partner, white-labeled, do not name externally)
- News API provider (white-labeled, do not name externally)
- Spotify audio partner name (white-labeled, do not name externally)
- Transmit Live (white-labeled, do not name externally). IRIS.TV and Spectrum Reach are the only
  externally nameable partners, and always lead with Sightly.

## Audience Framing (Internal Reference)

Three primary audience types Sightly sells to:

**Paid Media (Brand Direct and Agencies).** Executive sponsors are CEO/CMO/SVP level. Gatekeepers are VP/Director of Paid Media, Media Strategy, Performance. End users are Managers and Specialists. Pain points: budget constraints, platform fragmentation, manual reporting burden.

**Media and Advertising Agencies / Holding Companies.** Executive sponsors are CEO/CMO/Chief Investment Officers. Gatekeepers are VP/Director of Social, Programmatic, Media Planning. End users are Specialists and Traders. Pain points: staffing, manual processes, proving incrementality.

**Marketing and Brand Strategy (Brand Direct).** Executive sponsors are CEO/CMO/Chief Brand Officer. Gatekeepers are Directors of Marketing, Brand, Consumer Insights. End users are Marketing/Brand Managers. Pain points: proving marketing value to the C-suite, maintaining brand consistency across partners.

## Formatting by Output Type

- **Deck copy:** Presentation-ready lines that can be dropped into slides
- **Emails:** Prioritize readability and flow over long blocks of dense copy
- **Spreadsheet / persona-builder content:** Formatting easy to paste
- **Bullets:** Use when they improve readability; section headers when content breaks naturally
- **Structured writing, but not stiff**
